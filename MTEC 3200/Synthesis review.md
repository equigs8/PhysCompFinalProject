## Synthesis
Before we write our synthesis paragraph, we'll use two visual methods before writing:
1. **Affinity wall:** groups individual observations into patterns
2. **Journey map:** shows how one person moves through the problem over time


### Affinity mapping
An affinity wall is a set of sticky notes, one observation per note, grouped by similarity.
- Each note is a single quote or observation from one interview
- Groups are formed by looking at the notes, not by deciding categories first
- Each group is named from the participant's point of view


#### Studio Activity: Affinity wall

1. Write one quote or observation per sticky note
    a. Use the participant's words where possible
    b. Mark each note with the participant code: P1, P2, etc.
    c. Aim for at least 15 notes across all interviews
2. Plasce all notes on the wall
3. Group the notes
4. Names each group with an "I" statement

#### Naming your clusters
Name each cluster as an "I" statement in the participant's voice
A topic label says what teh notes are about. An "I" statement says what participants experience.

### Journey map
A journey map shows the steps a person takes through the problem, and how they feel at each step.
- Based on one participant's real story
- Steps run left to right, in order
- Feelings are written under each step
- The lowest point on the map is the design target


#### Studio Activity: Journey map
1. Choose one participant's story
2. Draw a horizontal line of steps, from the start of the situation to the end
3. Under each step, write what the participant did and how they felt
4. Draw a line connecting the feelings, high to low
5. Mark the lowest point

# Comparative Analysis

A comparative analysis reviews products that address a similar problem. These include direct competitors and any other product people use for the same need.
- Existing products show what people already use, and what they still find frustrating
- They reveal patterns worth borrowing, such as layouts, flows, and features
- They help you avoid building something that already exists

The goal is inspiration and understanding, not copying.

### Studio Activity: Comparative Analysis
1. Find 3 products related to your problem. Include at least one that your participants mentioned
    a. Use app stores, product websites, and [mobbin.com](https://mobbin.com/)
    b. Products may be websites, phone apps, or physical devices
2. For each product, note:
    a. What it does well
    b. What does not fit your participants, based on your interviews
    c. One pattern, flow, or feature worth borrowing
3. Choose one product and draw its system diagram: input, logic, data, output, and external servieces
